# SEOBNRv4 source specification: paper components 0.5.0

**Status: incomplete research specification.** This version establishes an
independent executable source of truth for the components marked `executable`
or `conditional` in [manifest.json](manifest.json). Entries marked `diagnostic`
check a published approximation or printed numerical values without defining
the historical SEOBNRv4 implementation. It is **not** a source of
truth for the full SEOBNRv4 generator, and it cannot qualify a new ROM. The
published fitted decimals are the authors' calibrated numbers, not numbers
generated by diffGW. The historic pilot ROM tables are not derived from these
equations and have no validated SEOBNRv4 provenance.

The primary source is [Bohé et al., *Phys. Rev. D* 95, 044028 (2017),
arXiv:1611.03703v3](https://arxiv.org/html/1611.03703). Equation numbers below
refer to that exact version. [Section VII](https://arxiv.org/html/1611.03703#S7)
describes a numerical surrogate built from the full time-domain model; it does
not print the trained SVD bases or parameter-spline coefficients. The official
external HDF5 is author data, not a derivation of the physical generator or an
asset we can label as independently generated.

## Scope, variables, and conventions

This scope is the dominant complex `(2,2)` mode for nonprecessing black-hole
binaries. Component masses are positive, `q=m1/m2>=1` in the paper, component
spins are aligned projections in `[-1,1]`, `M=m1+m2`, `mu=m1*m2/M`, and
`nu=mu/M` in `(0,1/4]`. The analytic fits use

`chi = (m1² chi1 + m2² chi2)/(m1²+m2²)` (Eq. 8).

This is **not** the mass-weighted `chi_eff` in the paper's Figure 1.
`published.mass_spin` is invariant under simultaneous body exchange and mass
rescaling. The paper's orbital time is in units of initial `M`, and the peak
lag `Delta t22_peak/M` is dimensionless. The backend adapter uses positive GW
frequency `omega22=-d arg(h22)/d(t/M)` and `h22=A exp(-i phase)`; this sign
choice is checked by the matching derivative below. The paper's Eq. (10) uses
`nu * A_tilde * exp(+i phi_tilde) exp(-i sigma t)`. It is converted by setting
`phi_tilde(0)=-phase(0)` and `sigma=omega_QNM-i gamma_QNM`, with positive
`omega_QNM` and damping `gamma_QNM`. The reference accepts the peak amplitude
of `h22` itself and removes the paper's `nu` factor internally; it never adds
another `nu` to a backend-normalized amplitude.

## Canonical equations and derivation

`published.calibration` preserves the printed decimals exactly as SymPy
rationals for `K`, `d_SO`, `d_SS`, and `Delta t22_peak/M` (Eqs. 25, 26, 29–32).
The nonspinning polynomials remain separate so the `chi=0` reduction is
testable. No six-significant-digit numerical-compatibility rounding is applied.

`hamiltonian.radial_potentials` independently evaluates the deformed-Kerr
radial-potential block in Bohé Eqs. (2)–(3), using the explicit
`Delta_0`–`Delta_5` and inverse-`D` equations in
[Steinhoff et al. (2016), Appendix A](https://arxiv.org/html/1608.01907).
The factor multiplying the logarithm is `nu`, as printed in Steinhoff's
Eq. (A1) and [Taracchini et al. (2014), Eq. (2)](https://arxiv.org/html/1311.2544).
Bohé's printed Eq. (2) omits that factor; omitting it also breaks the Kerr
test-mass limit. The scalar reference evaluates the paper coefficients in
mpmath at high precision, differentiates `Delta_t` independently, and uses
the continuous horizon-product limit at zero spin. The tortoise factor
`xi=sqrt(Delta_t Delta_r)/(r²+a²)` follows
[Pan et al. (2010), Eq. (28)](https://arxiv.org/html/0912.3466).
`hamiltonian.energy_map` checks Bohé Eq. (1), including the distinction
between the total real energy and reduced-mass-normalized binding energy.
`hamiltonian.nonspinning_effective_hamiltonian` also checks the deformed-metric
orbital Hamiltonian and 3PN tortoise-momentum quartic term at zero spin,
including the exact Schwarzschild test-mass limit. Its finite-mass-ratio
quartic prescription is conditional: [Pan et al. (2012), Eqs. (14)–(15)](https://arxiv.org/html/1202.0790)
describe 3PN truncation but print an extra radial factor in Eq. (15) that is
dimensionally inconsistent. The scalar comparison checks the stated truncated
choice, not the exact historical implementation. These checks do not validate
the spin-coupled effective Hamiltonian.

`flux.emitted_flux` independently evaluates Bohé Eq. (9) from supplied
**pre-NQC** positive-`m` mode amplitudes through `ell=8`:

`F = sum[(m Omega)² |D_L h_lm|²] / (8 pi)`.

The factor of two relative to the full `m=-ell...ell` sum accounts for the
negative-`m` partners. [Pan et al. (2011), Eqs. (3), (30)](https://arxiv.org/html/1006.0431)
provide the independent Newtonian check `F_22=(32/5) nu² Omega^(10/3)`.
Torch composes 35 supplied factorized amplitudes with this normalization,
but this reference does not yet verify each amplitude formula, the high-`ell`
spin truncation, or a JAX factorized-flux path. The JAX development assembler
currently uses a 1.5PN prototype flux and Newtonian `(2,2)` mode, so no
cross-backend whole-waveform SEOBNRv4 claim follows from these checks.

`published.peak_targets` encodes the NR/Teukolsky peak amplitude, amplitude
curvature, GW frequency, and frequency slope (Appendix A, Eqs. 33–44). For
the quadratic fits, the printed linear coefficient `A1` is held fixed and
`A0,A2` are solved so that the expressions equal the printed test-particle
and equal-mass fits at `nu=0.001` and `nu=0.25`. The peak **frequency** fit
instead uses its printed `nu=0` endpoint. The peak amplitude derivative is
zero by definition of the attachment peak.

`published.ringdown_shape` gives the four free fit coefficients
`c1f,c2f,d1f,d2f` (Eqs. 13, 14, 16, 17). `ringdown.h22` evaluates the paper's
Eqs. 10, 12, 15 directly with mpmath, conditional on supplied peak inputs
and fundamental remnant QNM. Let `A0=|h22(0)|`, `gamma=-Im(sigma)>0`,
`w=Re(sigma)`, and `w0=-d arg(h22)/dt` at the peak. Matching amplitude and
its first derivative gives

`c1c = gamma A0 cosh(c2f)²/(nu c1f)` and
`c2c = A0/nu - c1c tanh(c2f)`.

Matching phase and GW frequency gives `phi0_tilde=-phase0` and
`d1c=(w-w0)(1+d2f)/(d1f d2f)`. At `d2f=0`, Eq. (15) has a removable
singularity; its continuous limit is used. These constraints prove `h22(0)`
and its first derivative match the supplied inspiral peak for this component.
They do **not** establish that the current inspiral supplies the correct peak,
remnant, or QNM. The paper explicitly says the amplitude is only `C¹` at
attachment; no second-derivative continuity is claimed.

`remnant.final_mass_fraction` independently evaluates the aligned-spin
remnant-mass prescription named in Bohé et al. Sec. II.C. Bohé points to
[Taracchini et al. (2014)](https://arxiv.org/html/1311.2544), whose
merger-ringdown paragraph specifies the
[Barausse et al. (2012) Eq. (18)](https://arxiv.org/html/1206.3803)
formula with its equal-mass fit replaced by
[Hemberger et al. (2013)](https://arxiv.org/html/1305.5991)'s hyperbola.
Those earlier sources do not print the full unequal-mass expression.
[Ossokine et al. (2020), Eq. (21)](https://arxiv.org/html/2004.09442)
prints it and explicitly identifies it as the fit used in previous EOB
models. In particular, that equation specifies the unequal-mass spin
argument of the Hemberger term.
For `a=(m1² chi1+m2² chi2)/M²` and
`s=(m1² chi1+m2² chi2)/(m1²+m2²)`, the resulting radiated fraction is

`E_rad/M = nu(1-4nu)[1-E_ISCO(a)] + 16nu²[0.00258-0.07730/(s-1.6939)]`,

and `M_final/M=1-E_rad/M`. The test suite checks the algebraic identity
against Barausse's Eq. (18), the Hemberger equal-mass limit, and both tensor
frontends at nonspinning, unequal-mass, and aligned/antialigned-spin points.
The existing frontend formula agrees with this explicit 2020 restatement of
the earlier EOB prescription; its 2020 citation is not evidence of a changed
fit.
This is component-level transcription evidence only. The remnant-spin fit
choice and exact historical QNM interpolation remain open.

`remnant.final_spin_projection` evaluates the aligned-spin formula of
[Hofmann, Barausse and Rezzolla (2016), Eqs. (2)–(7), (11) and Table 1](https://arxiv.org/html/1605.01938)
using mpmath and the paper's printed coefficients. It requires an explicit
`fit_order` of `(1,2)`, `(3,3)`, or `(3,4)`; Eq. (11) fixes the unprinted
`k00` from the equal-mass, zero-spin value `0.68646`. Tests check that limit,
body exchange, mass-scale invariance, and each variant against Torch and JAX.
The SEOBNRv4 paper cites Hofmann but does not identify a Table 1 order.
Hofmann et al. regard `(1,2)` as the choice less prone to overfitting; the
development assembler currently chooses `(3,4)`. Component agreement does
not establish which variant defines SEOBNRv4, so model-level selection is
still pending.

## Fundamental QNM: published data and diagnostic

Bohé et al. Sec. II.C and Appendix B state that SEOBNRv4 obtains the remnant
mass and spin and then **interpolates numerical QNM results** from
[Berti, Cardoso and Will (2006), Appendix E](https://arxiv.org/html/gr-qc/0512160).
The papers do not specify the historical interpolation knots, precision, or
rule. An analytic fit to those results is therefore not an exact SEOBNRv4 QNM
prescription. The Berti numerical table and fitted coefficients are the
authors' numbers, not newly generated diffGW data.

Berti uses time dependence `exp(+i omega t)` and a positive imaginary frequency
`+1/tau`. Bohé's Eq. (10) uses `exp(-i sigma t)` with
`sigma=omega_R-i/tau`. Berti's quality factor is `Q=omega_R*tau/2`
(Eq. 1). For the corotating fundamental `(l,m,n)=(2,2,0)`, Berti's
Eqs. (73)–(74) and Table VIII print the approximate diagnostic fit

`F=M_f omega_R=1.5251-1.1568(1-j)^0.1292`,
`Q=0.7000+1.4187(1-j)^(-0.4990)`, and `M_f/tau=F/(2Q)`.

Its stated maximum frequency and quality-factor errors on `0<=j<=0.99` are
1.85% and 0.88%, respectively. `qnm.berti_220_fit` evaluates those printed
decimals, while `qnm.sigma_initial_mass_units` divides both frequency and
damping by `M_f/M` to use the initial-mass time of the waveform. Neither
function is selected as the model's QNM source. Berti Table II gives numerical
`(M_f omega_R,M_f/tau)` values `(0.3737,0.0890)` at `j=0`,
`(0.5860,0.0756)` at `j=0.8`, and `(0.8254,0.0386)` at `j=0.98`.
The native Torch/JAX Kerr equation solves agree with these at the table's
printed four-decimal precision. Their generated spline numbers are ours, but
this agreement does not establish equivalence to SEOBNRv4's historical
interpolation or qualify the full generator.

## Evidence and implementation map

The canonical [manifest](manifest.json) assigns stable IDs, exact paper
equations, reference functions, both backend functions, and maintained tests.
The test suite checks printed-fit endpoints, nonspinning reduction, body
exchange, symbolic continuity, selected high-precision ringdown samples, and
Torch/JAX outputs separately against the independent scalar reference. These
checks establish transcription and component agreement on tested points, not
whole-waveform parity or physical accuracy. The scalar reference imports no
Torch/JAX or production coefficient data. Increasing `mpmath` precision cannot
recover digits beyond the printed fit precision.

Install the development-only [reference requirements](requirements.txt) in a
clean environment. From the repository root, run `PYTHONPATH=src python -m
pytest -q dev/native_rom/reference/tests/test_published.py`, then run the
Torch and JAX test files in separate processes on macOS. The base Conda
environment on this machine loads duplicate OpenMP runtimes on Torch import;
the repository's virtual environment runs these checks. No package runtime
dependency or distribution asset changes in this branch.

## Promotion blockers

The manifest names each remaining component and its next discriminating task.
Most urgently, the remainder of the spin Hamiltonian and all factorized mode
amplitudes need equation review; the remnant-spin choice and QNM interpolation
details need independently documented provenance. The ODE initial conditions,
solver, event location, and output-grid coupling need a converged campaign.
The [first frozen generator campaign](../evidence/2026-09-22/README.md) failed
its direct convergence gate. The [tighter campaign and peak-anchored
whole-waveform diagnostic](../evidence/2026-09-23/README.md) still fail the
numerical gate. A controlled grid experiment in that evidence bundle attributes
most of the grid discrepancy for the nonspinning row to the moving coarse/fine
restart sample; it does not resolve the ODE-tolerance failure or change the
default algorithm. A still tighter three-level campaign also failed both
numerical gates with nonmonotonic errors. Its regional diagnostic finds a
phase-dominated ODE discrepancy already present before attachment, without
identifying its numerical cause. The whole-waveform diagnostic compares
only two rows and does not establish public-binary parity. The current generator
and ROM work therefore stay on this development branch. Reference completion,
frontend component checks, generator numerical convergence, public-binary
parity, and physical/NR holdouts are separate gates. No ROM training or release promotion follows
from this version alone.
