# SEOBNRv4 source specification: paper components 0.8.11

**Status: incomplete research specification.** This version establishes an
independent executable source of truth for the components marked `executable`
or `conditional` in [manifest.json](manifest.json). Entries marked `diagnostic`
check a published approximation or printed numerical values without defining
the historical SEOBNRv4 implementation. It is **not** a source of
truth for the full SEOBNRv4 generator, and it cannot qualify a new ROM. The
published fitted decimals are the authors' calibrated numbers, not numbers
generated by diffGW. The historic pilot ROM tables are not derived from these
equations and have no validated SEOBNRv4 provenance.

The primary source is [Bohé et al., *Phys. Rev. D* 95, 044028 (2017),
arXiv:1611.03703v3](https://arxiv.org/html/1611.03703). Equation numbers below
refer to that exact version. [Section VII](https://arxiv.org/html/1611.03703#S7)
describes a numerical surrogate built from the full time-domain model; it does
not print the trained SVD bases or parameter-spline coefficients. The official
external HDF5 is author data, not a derivation of the physical generator or an
asset we can label as independently generated.

## Inputs, conventions, and options

This scope is the dominant complex `(2,2)` mode for nonprecessing black-hole
binaries. Component masses are positive, `q=m1/m2>=1` in the paper, component
spins are aligned projections in `[-1,1]`, `M=m1+m2`, `mu=m1*m2/M`, and
`nu=mu/M` in `(0,1/4]`. The analytic fits use

`chi = (m1² chi1 + m2² chi2)/(m1²+m2²)` (Eq. 8).

This is **not** the mass-weighted `chi_eff` in the paper's Figure 1.
`published.mass_spin` is invariant under simultaneous body exchange and mass
rescaling. The paper's orbital time is in units of initial `M`, and the peak
lag `Delta t22_peak/M` is dimensionless. The backend adapter uses positive GW
frequency `omega22=-d arg(h22)/d(t/M)` and `h22=A exp(-i phase)`; this sign
choice is checked by the matching derivative below. The paper's Eq. (10) uses
`nu * A_tilde * exp(+i phi_tilde) exp(-i sigma t)`. It is converted by setting
`phi_tilde(0)=-phase(0)` and `sigma=omega_QNM-i gamma_QNM`, with positive
`omega_QNM` and damping `gamma_QNM`. The reference accepts the peak amplitude
of `h22` itself and removes the paper's `nu` factor internally; it never adds
another `nu` to a backend-normalized amplitude.

The research reference accepts dimensionless component masses and aligned
spins; development waveforms also specify a starting orbital frequency and
time grid. It does not promise a public time-to-frequency transform, a
production device or batching contract, or gradients through numerical
events. The separate official wrapper takes an external
`SEOBNRv4ROM_v3.0.hdf5` asset, version 3.0, with SHA-256
`4ff5317967d757fcad9323a74b75f4f63894ed87f4b1852d46cbe47cde488b9c`.
The [asset resolver](../../../src/torchwave/external_data.py) checks that
hash and consults explicit paths, configured data paths, or a user cache.
The asset is not generated by this source specification or bundled here; its
terms are those of the [author release](https://zenodo.org/records/14999310).

### Canonical equations and derivation

`published.calibration` preserves the printed decimals exactly as SymPy
rationals for `K`, `d_SO`, `d_SS`, and `Delta t22_peak/M` (Eqs. 25, 26, 29–32).
The nonspinning polynomials remain separate so the `chi=0` reduction is
testable. No six-significant-digit numerical-compatibility rounding is applied.

`hamiltonian.radial_potentials` independently evaluates the deformed-Kerr
radial-potential block in Bohé Eqs. (2)–(3), using the explicit
`Delta_0`–`Delta_5` and inverse-`D` equations in
[Steinhoff et al. (2016), Appendix A](https://arxiv.org/html/1608.01907).
The factor multiplying the logarithm is `nu`, as printed in Steinhoff's
Eq. (A1) and [Taracchini et al. (2014), Eq. (2)](https://arxiv.org/html/1311.2544).
Bohé's printed Eq. (2) omits that factor; omitting it also breaks the Kerr
test-mass limit. The scalar reference evaluates the paper coefficients in
mpmath at high precision, differentiates `Delta_t` independently, and uses
the continuous horizon-product limit at zero spin. The tortoise factor
`xi=sqrt(Delta_t Delta_r)/(r²+a²)` follows
[Pan et al. (2010), Eq. (28)](https://arxiv.org/html/0912.3466).
The pinned [historical LAL Hamiltonian source](https://git.ligo.org/lscsoft/lalsuite/-/raw/5154f04e470d1cc48d6f61193af728b5692474ab/lalsimulation/lib/LALSimIMRSpinEOBHamiltonian.c)
omits the `k5l u^4` contribution while forming an internal derivative of
the logarithmic radial potential. The potential value itself is the same.
The [frozen diagnostic](../evidence/2026-09-24/hamiltonian_log_derivative.json)
isolates that historical convention at sampled public states; it does not
alter this paper-derived scalar reference. A later compiled comparison at
eight identical states is documented in the
[campaign evidence](../evidence/2026-09-24/README.md).

`hamiltonian.energy_map` checks Bohé Eq. (1), including the distinction
between the total real energy and reduced-mass-normalized binding energy.
`V4-HAM-EQUATORIAL-PR0` composes the paper-derived aligned-spin metric and
spin terms at supplied `r,p_phi` with `p_r*=0`. The disputed tortoise quartic
vanishes there. Five zero-radial-momentum rows per frontend compare against
the independent scalar reference across unequal and equal masses, zero and
mixed spins, and varying radius and angular momentum. This is a value check
on supplied states, not a circular-orbit solution or a test of derivatives,
evolution, or historical-source fidelity; the source's radial-log derivative
choice remains different even at zero radial momentum.
`V4-ODE-RHS-PR0` applies the polar Hamilton equations of
[Buonanno--Damour (2000), Eqs. (24)--(27)](https://arxiv.org/html/gr-qc/0001013)
to that same high-precision scalar Hamiltonian. The tortoise conversion is
the one in [Pan et al. (2010), Eq. (28)](https://arxiv.org/html/0912.3466).
At supplied `p_r*=0`, the aligned Hamiltonian is even in radial momentum,
so `r_dot=0`; five-point scalar differentiation supplies the radius and
angular-momentum partials. A caller supplies the angular torque, and radial
dissipation is set to zero. Four rows compare all vector-field components
with Torch and JAX within `2e-10` relative and `2e-12` absolute. This leaf
does not establish nonzero-radial evolution, physical flux, initial-data
root finding, numerical convergence, or the historical derivative rule;
`V4-ODE-IC` remains pending.
`V4-ODE-RHS-SUPPLIED-FORCE` extends this scalar differentiation to four
supplied nonzero-`p_r*` states. It holds `p_r*` fixed when differentiating in
radius and checks the tortoise factors in both radial equations, using caller
supplied radial and angular dissipative forces. Torch and JAX agree with all
four independent scalar components within `2e-10` relative and `2e-12`
absolute. This establishes only a local vector-field leaf, not the physical
force prescription or integrated inspiral.
`hamiltonian.nonspinning_effective_hamiltonian` also checks the deformed-metric
orbital Hamiltonian and 3PN tortoise-momentum quartic term at zero spin,
including the exact Schwarzschild test-mass limit. Its finite-mass-ratio
quartic prescription follows the 3PN truncation in
[Pan et al. (2012), Eqs. (14)–(15)](https://arxiv.org/html/1202.0790),
although the printed Eq. (15) includes an extra radial factor that is
dimensionally inconsistent. The scalar comparison checks the stated truncated
choice. A [read-only audit of the pinned v6.2.1 source](../evidence/2026-09-23/historical_v4_component_audit.json)
finds that ordinary SEOBNRv4 selects the same choice; compiled component
parity was subsequently established for the compiled source and wheel wrapper
at eight explicit aligned-spin states. The
[same-state diagnostic](../evidence/2026-09-24/hamiltonian_same_state.json)
and [two-factor convention result](../evidence/2026-09-24/hamiltonian_source_conventions.json)
show that the paper-derived native Hamiltonian differs from that source, while
the development-only source-convention variant agrees to floating-point
precision. This does not qualify full dynamics or waveform generation.
The [expanded same-state result](../evidence/2026-09-25/hamiltonian_domain_states.json)
and [factorial replay](../evidence/2026-09-25/hamiltonian_domain_conventions.json)
extend that attribution to four mixed-spin late and four high-spin q=8 states.
The pinned source and wheel differ by at most `8.9e-16` in `H_real/nu`;
the published native value differs by at most `1.14e-5` absolute, and the
development variant with both source conventions agrees within `8.9e-16`.
The [predeclared boundary grid](../evidence/2026-09-25/hamiltonian_boundary_grid.json)
adds 12 explicit nonspinning, antialigned, and near-extreme-spin inputs;
source and wheel agree bitwise, and the [two-factor replay](../evidence/2026-09-25/hamiltonian_boundary_conventions.json)
closes the native gap to `1.8e-15` absolute. These states do not establish
orbit or waveform parity.
Changing only the diagnostic Hamiltonian calibration decimals to the pinned
source values lowers the aligned-spin peak-fitted full-waveform complex L2
from `0.00299749` to `0.00271301` and passes the three-tolerance ODE check;
the [frozen result](../evidence/2026-09-24/source_calibration_waveform.json)
is a source-convention attribution, not a change to this paper reference.
The [two boundary-row result](../evidence/2026-09-24/source_calibration_domain.json)
passes numerical convergence but slightly worsens both peak-fitted public
waveform comparisons. The [initial-state result](../evidence/2026-09-24/source_calibration_initial.json)
finds an unresolved inward-momentum discrepancy after aligning circular
radius and angular momentum. Neither observation promotes the source decimals
to an independently derived calibration.

`hamiltonian.effective_spin_mapping` checks the aligned, zero-gauge
3.5PN mapping in [Barausse and Buonanno (2011), Eqs. (50)–(52)](https://arxiv.org/html/1107.2904)
with the calibrated `d_SO nu S_Kerr/r³` term stated by
[Taracchini et al. (2014)](https://arxiv.org/html/1311.2544).
`hamiltonian.aligned_effective_hamiltonian` evaluates the equatorial
geodesic and spin terms in [Barausse and Buonanno (2010), Eqs. (161)–(164)](https://arxiv.org/html/0912.3517),
including Taracchini's `d_SS nu (S1²+S2²)/r⁴` addition and Bohé's radial
potential. Both tensor frontends agree with this separate high-precision
scalar evaluation at the tested aligned-spin points; the nonspinning limit,
body exchange, and mass-scale invariance also hold. The full Hamiltonian
check inherits the paper's finite-mass-ratio tortoise-quartic ambiguity above, and
component agreement does not validate the resulting dynamics or prove
historical implementation parity beyond the tested states.

`flux.emitted_flux` independently evaluates [Bohé Eq. (9)](https://arxiv.org/html/1611.03703)
from supplied **pre-NQC** positive-`m` mode amplitudes through `ell=8`:

`F = sum[(m Omega)² |D_L h_lm|²] / (8 pi)`.

Eq. (9) gives the angular-momentum radiation reaction; circular balance
converts its flux magnitude to emitted energy flux by a factor of `Omega`.
Equal squared amplitudes for the positive- and negative-`m` partners give
the factor of two relative to its full `m=-ell...ell` sum.
[Pan et al. (2011), Eqs. (3), (30)](https://arxiv.org/html/1006.0431)
provide the independent Newtonian check `F_22=(32/5) nu² Omega^(10/3)`.
The Torch and JAX positive-`m` sums agree with the scalar normalization for
supplied complex modes; JAX also passes batch, JIT, and Newtonian `(2,2)`
gradient checks. This executable component does not generate the modes.

For the still-pending `V4-FLUX-4-9` aggregate, a separate JAX factorized
engine generates all 35 declared pre-NQC moduli but is not wired into a
complete JAX SEOBNRv4 waveform. Its same-state Hamiltonian adapter produces
reduced-mass radiation-reaction forces that agree with the Torch adapter at
tested scalar points and pass JAX batch, JIT, and gradient checks; this is a
frontend check, not historical torque parity.

`published.ringdown_shape` gives the four free fit coefficients
`c1f,c2f,d1f,d2f` (Eqs. 13, 14, 16, 17). `ringdown.h22` evaluates the paper's
Eqs. 10, 12, 15 directly with mpmath, conditional on supplied peak inputs
and fundamental remnant QNM. Let `A0=|h22(0)|`, `gamma=-Im(sigma)>0`,
`w=Re(sigma)`, and `w0=-d arg(h22)/dt` at the peak. Matching amplitude and
its first derivative gives

`c1c = gamma A0 cosh(c2f)²/(nu c1f)` and
`c2c = A0/nu - c1c tanh(c2f)`.

Matching phase and GW frequency gives `phi0_tilde=-phase0` and
`d1c=(w-w0)(1+d2f)/(d1f d2f)`. At `d2f=0`, Eq. (15) has a removable
singularity; its continuous limit is used. These constraints prove `h22(0)`
and its first derivative match the supplied inspiral peak for this component.
They do **not** establish that the current inspiral supplies the correct peak,
remnant, or QNM. The paper explicitly says the amplitude is only `C¹` at
attachment; no second-derivative continuity is claimed.

### Fundamental QNM: published data and diagnostic

Bohé et al. Sec. II.C and Appendix B state that SEOBNRv4 obtains the remnant
mass and spin and then **interpolates numerical QNM results** from
[Berti, Cardoso and Will (2006), Appendix E](https://arxiv.org/html/gr-qc/0512160).
The papers do not specify the historical interpolation knots, precision, or
rule. The hash-pinned LAL source does: its
`XLALSimIMREOBGenerateQNMFreqV2fromFinal` function prints 107 spin knots and
fundamental `(2,2)` frequency/damping entries, then applies GSL
`gsl_interp_cspline` on `[-0.9996,0.9996]`. An analytic fit to those results
is therefore not an exact SEOBNRv4 QNM prescription. The source table, Berti
numerical data, and fitted coefficients are third-party numbers, not newly
generated diffGW data or production native assets.
The authors also host an [l=2 numerical data archive](https://blackholes.tecnico.ulisboa.pt/Webpagecodes/l2.tar.gz),
which supplies an independent numerical source; it is not bundled with diffGW.
The downloaded archive's SHA-256 is
`0569faae28e14366480cf594ea398f3d33ac9d3f82dbbddd43a68d920d807086`.
The archive's `n1l2m2.dat` indexes the fundamental as `n1` and reports the
frequency and signed damping at spin `0`, `0.8`, and `0.98` to roughly ten
decimal places. Three sparse values in `qnm.BERTI_AUTHOR_220_SAMPLES` are
author numbers used only for validation, with no table asset redistributed.

Berti uses time dependence `exp(+i omega t)` and a positive imaginary frequency
`+1/tau`. Bohé's Eq. (10) uses `exp(-i sigma t)` with
`sigma=omega_R-i/tau`. Berti's quality factor is `Q=omega_R*tau/2`
(Eq. 1). For the corotating fundamental `(l,m,n)=(2,2,0)`, Berti's
Eqs. (73)–(74) and Table VIII print the approximate diagnostic fit

`F=M_f omega_R=1.5251-1.1568(1-j)^0.1292`,
`Q=0.7000+1.4187(1-j)^(-0.4990)`, and `M_f/tau=F/(2Q)`.

Its stated maximum frequency and quality-factor errors on `0<=j<=0.99` are
1.85% and 0.88%, respectively. `qnm.berti_220_fit` evaluates those printed
decimals, while `qnm.sigma_initial_mass_units` divides both frequency and
damping by `M_f/M` to use the initial-mass time of the waveform. Neither
function is selected as the model's QNM source. Berti Table II gives numerical
`(M_f omega_R,M_f/tau)` values `(0.3737,0.0890)` at `j=0`,
`(0.5860,0.0756)` at `j=0.8`, and `(0.8254,0.0386)` at `j=0.98`.
The native Torch/JAX Kerr equation solves agree with the higher-precision
author archive values at those same three spins to `1e-9` absolute. Their
generated spline numbers are ours, but
this agreement does not establish equivalence to SEOBNRv4's historical
interpolation or qualify the full generator.
The older Berti archive diverges from the independent Kerr solve near extremal
spin: at `j=0.9999`, its frequency and positive damping exceed the native
values by `1.0152e-7` and `3.3590e-7`. A later, high-accuracy
[Cook (2019) author dataset](https://zenodo.org/records/2650358),
`KerrQNM_00.h5` dataset `/n00/m+02/{2,2,0}`, agrees with the native raw
Torch spline at `j=0.99990625` to `2.7e-13` in frequency and `6.8e-13` in
damping. Its archive MD5 is `34c04f3b03bbb99935d1f91f7296bd02` and
SHA-256 is `d5853acabc1a07b66b4fae437c02e1435a9d2b3561bd7e4752b9721c9cd48923`.
At `j=0.999`, both frontends also match a Cook author row within `3e-16`.
The [prospective full-table check](../evidence/2026-09-25/qnm_cook_domain.json)
uses every Cook author row within `|j| <= 0.9999`: 1,014 corotating and
1,012 counterrotating rows. Maximum frequency/damping differences are

not generator inputs or packaged assets. The signed-spin Torch/JAX frontends

The conditional `V4-QNM-NATIVE-220-SPLINE` leaf now specifies the *diffGW*

negative knot `j=-0.9996` it is `0.02135`. The separate
[Cook author-data check](../evidence/2026-09-24/qnm_negative_author.json)
finds native counterrotating `(2,-2,0)` roots within `7.0e-13` of five
independent author rows from `j=-0.99` through `-0.999625`; the wheel's
frequency differs from the author value by `0.01502` at `j=-0.9995`.
Three of those sparse author rows also check both Torch and JAX in the
reference test suite. The wheel's extreme negative-spin entries are not a
physical oracle. Neither dense author-row agreement nor historical spline
reproduction qualifies the waveform or justifies importing third-party
tables into the native generator.
The [post-hoc binary-input witnesses](../evidence/2026-09-24/qnm_binary_reachability.json)
confirm that the discrepancy is reachable through the remnant fit, which
agrees with the pinned public `SEOBNRv4` remnant helper to `2.3e-16` on ten
rows. At the [Bohé et al. (2017), Sec. III](https://arxiv.org/html/1611.03703)
NR calibration configuration `(q,chi1,chi2)=(8,0.85,0.85)`, the remnant spin
is `0.8941271984` and native/wheel QNM frequency differs by `1.20e-6`,
above the prior `6e-7` proximity gate. At `(76,1,-1)`, outside the explicitly
NR-calibrated configurations, the difference is `1.91e-5` at remnant spin
`0.9995469946`. The `(80,1,-1)` remnant spin `0.9996053273` exceeds the
pinned public QNM helper's upper limit. Native values at the first two rows
match strict original Kerr equation solves to `3.4e-16`, but this is not
independent external validation or a whole-waveform comparison. A release
domain and any deliberate historical-interpolation compatibility mode remain
to be specified and tested separately.

## Implementation mapping

The canonical [manifest](manifest.json) assigns stable IDs, exact paper
equations, reference functions, both backend functions, and maintained tests.
The test suite checks printed-fit endpoints, nonspinning reduction, body
exchange, symbolic continuity, selected high-precision ringdown samples, and
Torch/JAX outputs separately against the independent scalar reference. These
checks establish transcription and component agreement on tested points, not
whole-waveform parity or physical accuracy. The scalar reference imports no
Torch/JAX or production coefficient data. Increasing `mpmath` precision cannot
recover digits beyond the printed fit precision.

The scalar reference is independent of production Torch/JAX coefficient
arrays. The native frontends implement their own expressions, with historical
carrier deviations and opt-in diagnostics documented alongside each
component. An executable reference component is not a public approximant
option; the development generator is not registered by this specification.

## Evidence and limitations

Source transcription, scalar checks, frontend agreement, pinned-binary
comparisons, and physical validation are separate claims. The
[campaign record](../evidence/2026-09-24/README.md) gives inputs, hashes,
metrics, and failed gates for the numerical and whole-waveform diagnostics.
The [wheel spline capture](../evidence/2026-09-24/wheel_nqc_spline_capture.json)
replays its private NQC fit within `1.01e-12` coefficient L2 on three rows,
while an exact pinned-source adapter reproduces its own generator inputs.
The wheel's pre-NQC amplitude samples differ from source evaluation on the
same states by at most `1.61e-10` around matching and account for most of the
remaining coefficient gap. Feeding the wheel's captured orbital frequency to
the source mode closes the same-state phase difference to `3.55e-15`; the
amplitude still differs by up to `8.38e-11`. Source and pinned-wheel
Hamiltonian-wrapper values agree with the source mode's Hamiltonian to
`8.88e-16` in `H/nu` on the same matching-window states; the wheel generator's
private argument was not captured. The residual numerical origin and an
independent, validated NQC rule remain unresolved.
The later [two-row conditioned-FD intervention](../evidence/2026-09-25/hamiltonian_public_fd_intervention.json)
changes only the development generator's source-backed Hamiltonian decimals
and logarithmic derivative. Its physically time/phase-fitted public residuals
fall from `0.004031` to `0.002534` for aligned spin and from `0.002650` to
`0.000986` for antialigned spin. The remaining differences and absent
domain-wide convergence keep `V4-HAM-1-3` and `V4-TD-FD-CONVENTIONS` pending.
The [frozen factorial follow-up](../evidence/2026-09-25/hamiltonian_fd_factorial.json)
isolates the historical logarithmic derivative as the main contributor on
both selected rows: its standalone residuals are `0.002492` and `0.001190`.
The shorter source calibration decimals alone leave residuals of `0.004071`
and `0.002650`, and have opposite incremental effects when combined with the
derivative. These observations identify a source-convention effect, not a
domain-wide or independent-physics validation.
The [two-row derivative extension](../evidence/2026-09-25/hamiltonian_fd_derivative_extension.json)
leaves the q=4 nonspinning FD waveform bitwise unchanged and slightly raises
the mixed-spin fitted residual from `0.000559` to `0.000577`. It rules out a
uniform improvement claim across the four selected FD cases.
The later [q8 raw-phase decomposition](../evidence/2026-09-24/source_q8_raw_phase.json)
finds same-state pre-NQC phase parity to `2.9e-14 rad` when the native mode
uses the pinned source frequency. At `-25 M` relative to a `-100 M` gauge,
the native/source trajectory term (`+0.001882 rad`) offsets part of the NQC
term (`-0.004345 rad`). The remaining `0.000327 rad` between reconstructed
and full-output mode differences closes as separate native and source
reconstruction errors. A post-hoc [output-interpolation control](../evidence/2026-09-24/source_q8_output_interpolation.json)
reduces the source-side error at `-25 M` to `-7.31e-8 rad` with cubic phase
interpolation. The [fine-trajectory control](../evidence/2026-09-24/source_q8_fine_phase.json)
accounts for `+0.000323039 rad` of the native-side reconstruction by using
the attachment's internal fine states. Together they reduce the pair
remainder at that probe to `2.07e-7 rad`. The full-domain dynamics and
physical NQC prescription remain open; neither control qualifies the model.
The [current peak-preserving q8 tolerance replay](../evidence/2026-09-24/peak_condition_q8_tighter_ode.json)
matches its frozen baseline hash and passes one tighter ODE step at `0.5 M`
with `3.4133e-7` direct complex relative L2 and unchanged event times.
This is one-row numerical evidence, not domain or physical qualification.
The [q8 output-grid replay](../evidence/2026-09-24/peak_condition_q8_grid.json)
likewise preserves all nested mode samples when the requested step halves
from `0.5 M` to `0.25 M` at that tighter tolerance. Both runs retain the same
internal `0.015625 M` step; wider numerical and physical gates remain open.

### Promotion blockers

The manifest names each remaining component and its next discriminating task.
Most urgently, the historical `(2,1)` higher-order spin hybrid, high-`ell`
mode choices, and QNM compatibility/domain decisions remain open. The pinned source
audit identifies the Hamiltonian's finite-mass-ratio quartic choice, but its
compiled component comparison is pending. The ODE initial conditions,
solver, event location, and output-grid coupling need converged campaigns across
the intended parameter domain.
The [first frozen generator campaign](../evidence/2026-09-22/README.md) failed
its direct convergence gate. The default [tighter campaign and peak-anchored
whole-waveform diagnostic](../evidence/2026-09-23/README.md) still fail the
numerical gate. An opt-in local-derivative, fixed-restart diagnostic passes both
numerical gates for one nonspinning row, but retains a `0.02171` sampled-peak
complex relative L2 difference against the public binary. Sub-sample peak
fitting reduces that metric to about `0.0068–0.0069` without a relative time
or phase optimization. Its post-hoc choices and single converged row do not
qualify the model. An earlier controlled grid
experiment in that evidence bundle attributes most of the grid discrepancy for
the nonspinning row to the moving coarse/fine restart sample; that experiment
alone did not resolve the ODE-tolerance failure or change the default algorithm.
A still tighter three-level campaign also failed both
numerical gates with nonmonotonic errors. Its regional diagnostic finds a
phase-dominated ODE discrepancy already present before attachment. A separate
cross-integrator check through `314 M` finds accepted states stable to about
`1e-10` against DOP853, while the natural-cubic output sampler differs by up
to `1.30e-6 rad` in the earlier `165 M` check. Opt-in Hermite resampling
reduces that sampler discrepancy but still fails the original full-waveform
ODE and grid convergence gates (`6.833e-6` and `6.553e-6`, respectively,
against a `1e-6` complex-error budget). The Hermite error diagnostic
attributes the remaining Hermite-route phase sensitivity for the tested
nonspinning row chiefly to changes in the NQC phase-basis derivative matrix:
holding the calibrated phase coefficients fixed reduces the refined-pair
boundary-phase difference from `1.34e-5` to `6.65e-9 rad`. That numerical
finding is not an independent validation of the historical NQC prescription.
Local-polynomial differentiation of the same fine-grid basis reduces the
cross-tolerance coefficient spread by about an order of magnitude, but its
absolute coefficients depend on the differentiation window and order; no
replacement rule has been selected. A separate DOP853 dense-trajectory check
of the same provisional equations places the tight-run natural-cubic NQC
phase-basis derivative matrix about `1.40e-7` from its five-point estimate,
versus `2.99e-9` for a local polynomial matrix. This isolates a numerical
derivative discrepancy without resolving historical calibration conventions.
An opt-in ±`0.5 M`, degree-six local derivative experiment passes the original
ODE tolerance gate on that row (`6.52e-7` medium-to-fine complex relative L2),
but its output-grid error increases from `2.46e-8` to `6.61e-7`, so the grid
gate fails. That window was selected after inspecting the same row's DOP853
diagnostic; it is not an independently validated model rule. Peak-anchored
whole-waveform complex relative L2 remains `0.02171096` nonspinning and
`0.01335888` for one exploratory aligned-spin row. With the restart fixed at
`165 M` on all grids, the nonspinning output-grid error decreases from
`2.46e-8` to `4.78e-9`; its peak-anchored LAL difference is still `0.02171172`.
The same fixed-restart campaign on an aligned-spin row fails its ODE complex
and amplitude budgets (`3.387e-6` and `1.466e-6`) and its grid monotonicity
gate (complex error increases from `1.946e-8` to `1.114e-7`). The paired
sampled-peak LAL difference is `0.01336093`, reduced to about `0.0078–0.0081`
by sub-sample peak fitting, with a failed numerical prerequisite.
Frozen-hash replays of that aligned-spin row find pre-NQC mode discrepancies
of `2.608e-9` (ODE) and `3.620e-10` (grid), much smaller than the finished
waveform discrepancies. The shared fine-run NQC coefficients already amplify
the difference on the coarse prefix. The amplitude and phase matching systems
have condition numbers near `833` and `8.52` with roundoff-level solve
residuals; the sampled input responsible for coefficient drift remains
unresolved in that first replay. A [matching-input attribution](../evidence/2026-09-23/README.md)
reconstructs both frozen NQC systems and closes their coefficient changes to
roundoff. In both refined ODE and grid pairs, the amplitude basis matrix
contributes about 86% of the coefficient-difference vector norm, predominantly
through the second-derivative row. The phase RHS contributes about 107% and
99% of the phase coefficient-difference norm in the ODE and grid pairs,
respectively; its second-derivative row dominates, with a small opposing
matrix contribution in the ODE pair. These are algebraic attributions under
the selected numerical discretization, not evidence for changing the
published NQC targets or a validated differentiation rule.
A subsequent [two-row dense-output check](../evidence/2026-09-23/nqc_second_derivatives_two_rows.json)
finds that a fixed local polynomial reduces the amplitude-product and
waveform-phase second-derivative discrepancy against DOP853 under the same
provisional equations. The [predeclared opt-in test](../evidence/2026-09-23/nqc_second_derivative_campaign.json)
passes the ODE tolerance gate on both rows and both gates on the nonspinning
row, but fails aligned-spin grid monotonicity (`5.69e-11` to `4.89e-10`). Its
[peak-anchored whole-waveform comparison](../evidence/2026-09-23/nqc_second_derivative_waveforms.json)
remains near the frozen `0.00695` nonspinning and `0.00807` aligned-spin complex
relative L2 values. The candidate is an unvalidated numerical experiment, not
the historical SEOBNRv4 NQC prescription.
An [exact-hash replay](../evidence/2026-09-23/nqc_grid_floor.json) finds a
`3.62e-10` raw pre-NQC inspiral difference in the failed refined grid pair,
so matching alone cannot account for the `4.89e-10` whole-waveform difference.
A [controlled replay](../evidence/2026-09-23/nqc_grid_initial_step.json) fixes
the coarse ODE's first trial step at `0.5 M` while keeping the candidate and
`165 M` restart unchanged; all three output grids then agree exactly on nested
samples. For this aligned-spin row, the failed frozen grid monotonicity is
attributable to the solver's default first-step dependence on output spacing.
The control reuses one numerical trajectory and does not constitute an
independent convergence-order or physical-accuracy check. The original gate
remains failed.
With quadratic-power peak fits, the corresponding nonspinning difference is
`0.006949`. In a replay of the same two frozen waveforms, inspiral
(`t<-20 M`) squared complex error is 94.07%/96.19% phase-related
(nonspinning/aligned), with time-dependent residual phase and frequency.
This does not distinguish initial-data, Hamiltonian, flux, waveform-phase,
or numerical causes. The [regional evidence](../evidence/2026-09-23/README.md)
records the bins and the separate LAL source audit. In particular, the LAL
v4 trajectory begins with zero NQC flux coefficients; omitting a nonzero NQC
factor from the native inspiral flux cannot explain this early residual.
A [six-row whole-trajectory cross-integrator
check](../evidence/2026-09-23/rkf45_full_trajectory.json) now passes its
predeclared diagnostic limits for the fixed-restart fine-stage sampler.
The largest native state difference is `1.61e-11`, while two tight DOP853
references differ by up to `2.47e-10`. This strengthens numerical evidence
for that provisional ODE; it does not resolve the physical or whole-waveform
discrepancies above.
An opt-in fully native start from `M Omega=10^(-3/2)` reduces the two-row
peak-fitted LAL differences to about `5.02e-4` and `1.69e-3`. Its frozen
[convergence campaign](../evidence/2026-09-23/inline_warmstart_convergence.json)
passes both numerical checks on the nonspinning row but fails the aligned-spin
ODE complex and amplitude budgets (`3.71e-6` and `1.50e-6` against `1e-6`).
The start-state correction is therefore still a development diagnostic.
The separate public-binary whole-waveform comparison
compares only two rows and does not establish public-binary parity. The current
generator and ROM work therefore stay on this development branch. Reference
completion, frontend component checks, generator numerical convergence,
public-binary parity, and physical/NR holdouts are separate gates. No ROM
training or release promotion follows
from this version alone.

## Reproduce the checks

Install the development-only [reference requirements](requirements.txt) in a
clean environment. From the repository root, run
`PYTHONPATH=src:. python -m pytest -q dev/native_rom/reference/tests/test_published.py`;
run `test_torch.py` and `test_jax.py` in separate processes on macOS. The
repository virtual environment avoids duplicate OpenMP runtimes seen in the
base Conda environment. Run
`PYTHONPATH=src:. python -m pytest -q dev/native_rom/tests/test_qualification.py`
for the qualification runner. The three-row private-fit replay uses the exact
pinned source, header, wheel, and interposer hashes specified in
[its frozen plan](../evidence/2026-09-24/wheel_nqc_spline_capture_plan.json);
its command and limitations are in the [campaign record](../evidence/2026-09-24/README.md).
Changes to equations, conventions, numerical rules, or external-asset versions
require a new manifest/specification version and fresh frozen evidence before
any release-catalog change. No package runtime dependency or distribution
asset is introduced by this research specification.
