# Clean NNLO precession series

The standalone evaluator transcribes Pratten et al., arXiv:2004.06503v2 Appendix G.1, Eqs. (120)--(122j). The series are
\(\alpha=\sum_{i=-3}^1\alpha_i(\pi Mf)^{i/3}+\alpha_{\log}\log(\pi Mf)\) and the same form for epsilon, with the listed coefficient rows (122a--e) and (122f--j). Variables are the paper's Eqs. (114)--(119): dimensionless component masses, \(\delta=\sqrt{1-4\eta}\), and \(\chi_\ell=m_1\chi_{1\ell}+m_2\chi_{2\ell}\). The effective in-plane parameter is Eq. (22), using Eq. (20)/(21) for \(S_p\).

The evaluator takes \(v=(\pi Mf)^{1/3}\), omits integration constants, and returns `(alpha, epsilon)`. Frame anchoring and the relation \(\gamma=-\epsilon\) are handled by the Appendix C/Table IV convention and are not silently inferred from an oracle. The diagnostic uses native `evaluate_msa_precession(..., prec_version=102, convention=7)` only as a black-box comparison.

Across four independent mass/spin systems, clean alpha evolution relative to 40 Hz agrees with native alpha to at most 7.1e-15 rad; clean epsilon evolution agrees with `-native gamma` to at most 1.1e-14 rad. This isolates the earlier apparent discrepancy to sign/constant convention (gamma is minus epsilon and absolute offsets are frame choices), rather than an NNLO coefficient error. It does not claim agreement with the public XPP angle gauge, whose reference-frame anchoring is separate.

## Public angle comparison

The public black-box call is `SimIMRPhenomXPPNAngles(REAL8Vector freqs, REAL8 m1_SI, REAL8 m2_SI, chi1x, chi1y, chi1z, chi2x, chi2y, chi2z, inclination, fRef_In, mprime, Dict)`. With SI masses and `mprime=2`, its alpha evolution relative to 40 Hz differs materially from the clean/native series in unequal-mass systems; for the 40+20 reference case, public deltas are `[-3.030747,0,2.564519,3.800488]` at `[25,40,80,160]` Hz, versus native/clean `[-4.366113,0,3.625736,5.311840]`. Equal masses are close (`[-5.045483,0,4.217361,6.256897]` public versus `[-4.912326,0,4.084917,6.048345]` native).

This mismatch survives reference anchoring and is not explained by gamma/epsilon sign. It is therefore a public-binary implementation or contract difference outside the published Appendix-G coefficient series; no correction is selected from this residual. The diagnostic records public versions 101--104 and all four independent systems for follow-up.

A 24-point least-squares identification over the published basis `{v^-3,v^-2,v^-1,v,log(v^3),1}` reconstructs the public samples at held-out frequencies to `<=1.4e-12` rad. The leading public alpha coefficient is exactly reproduced by the Appendix-G expression after changing only the mass-difference sign to `delta=(m2-m1)/M`: for 40+20, public `alpha_-3=-0.1432291667`, while the paper-sign transcription gives `-0.221354...`. This pattern repeats for 30+10 (`-0.1302083`) and 50+5 (`-0.1119792`), with equal masses unchanged. Public epsilon has the corresponding opposite sign convention in the returned second angle. This is strong evidence of a component-label/delta convention mismatch between the public API and the native evaluator, while the exact higher-order mapping remains a separate parameter-contract question. It is a diagnostic inference only; no coefficient is substituted into production.

A corrected all-coefficient comparison shows the public parameterization is algebraically explained by *both* conventions: `delta=(m2-m1)/M` and `chi_l=(m1*chi1z+m2*chi2z)/m1` (the single-spin effective reduction discussed in Sec. IV.1), while retaining the paper's normalized component mass `m1/M` in the coefficients. With the returned public second angle compared as `-epsilon`, all five nonconstant coefficients agree across the four systems to approximately `1e-6` or better (the 50+5 alpha fit is limited by basis conditioning; sample/held-out residuals remain <=3e-14). Thus the earlier conclusion that the public binary implementation violates the paper is withdrawn: the native/public gap is a mass-difference and effective-spin input-contract mismatch.

The leading-order convention can also be derived independently: with \(\Omega_p=(2+3m_2/(2m_1))\eta v^5/M\) and \(dt/dv=5M(32\eta)^{-1}v^{-9}\), integration gives \(\alpha_{-3}=-(5/96)(2+3m_2/(2m_1))\), equal to Eq. (122a) when the public delta sign is used. A process-local XP adapter applying the public-contract series while retaining native geometry and reference anchors changes the full waveform substantially (relative L2: hp 1.10–1.60, hc 1.16–1.60 across four systems). This is an isolated sensitivity experiment, not evidence for integration; frame/source-angle conventions and public waveform parity still require separate validation.

The process-local adapter computes the anchor at the exact scalar reference velocity and returns `gamma=-(epsilon-epsilon_ref)+gamma_ref`. Paired external comparisons use the public `SimInspiralChooseFDWaveform` API with explicit `SimInspiralWaveformParamsInsertPhenomXPrecVersion(..., 102)` and convention 1 and are recorded in `precession-nnlo-paired.json`. For XP, baseline plus/cross raw relative-L2 is 0.04865/0.06117 (catalog) and 0.30959/0.27865 (holdout); the clean angle substitution gives 1.72057/1.71744 and 0.94376/0.91743 respectively. Two additional independent XP systems are included in the JSON (40+20 and 30+10). For Pv2, the existing model route does not call the MSA adapter, so baseline and substitution are identical; its paired metrics are 0.25229/0.25723 (catalog) and 1.41831/1.34002 (holdout). These are diagnostic metrics only; no runtime coefficients are substituted.
